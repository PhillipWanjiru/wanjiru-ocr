# Interview guide

Semi-structured, 45 to 60 minutes. Used with `plan.md` and after `consent-form.md` has been signed.

**Rules of the room.** Ask about the last time something happened, not about what people usually do or would
do. Ask "how" and "what" before "why". Stay silent for five seconds after an answer; people fill silence
with the interesting part. Never suggest an answer, never defend a product, never say what the project is
before the closing section. If they ask what it is, say "I'll tell you at the end, so as not to colour what
you say".

Tags in the margin: **L** listen for, **P** probes. Observations are written down as *said*, *seen* or
*inferred*.

## 0. Opening (5 minutes)

Thank them. Read the first paragraph of the consent form aloud and ask whether the recording can start.
Say: "There are no right answers. I am interested in how you actually work, including the messy parts. You
will not hurt my feelings by saying something is bad or pointless."

## A. The person and the work (5 minutes)

1. Tell me about your job and what a normal week looks like. **P** How long have you done this? What other
   people are involved?
2. Which documents come across your desk, and about how many in a week? **L** classes, volume, peaks.

## B. The last batch (15 minutes), the centre of the interview

3. Think of the last time you had a pile of documents to get into the system. Walk me through it from the
   moment they arrived. **P** What was the first thing you did? What did you do next? Where were the
   documents? What was on the screen? **L** every handoff, every tool, every place where something is
   copied by hand.
4. Where did it take longest? **P** What were you waiting for? What did you have to look up?
5. Did anything have to be done twice? **P** Why? How did you find out?

## C. When it goes wrong (10 minutes)

6. Tell me about a time a wrong number got through. **P** How was it found, and by whom? What did it cost
   (money, time, an awkward conversation)? What changed afterwards? **L** the real price of an error;
   whether a wrong value that nobody noticed is worse than a delay.
7. How do you decide whether a value needs a second look? **P** What do you look at? Is there a value you
   always check? One you never check? **L** the cues people really use; this is where trust is made.
8. What do you do with a document you cannot read? **P** Who do you ask?

## D. Tools (5 minutes)

9. What do you use to get the contents of a document into the system now? **P** What do you like about
   it? What do you put up with? Did you choose it? **L** switching costs, what "good enough" means.
10. Have you tried anything that reads documents automatically? **P** What happened? Why did you stop or
    carry on? **L** the real reasons, usually trust and cleanup effort, not accuracy figures.

## E. Review and responsibility (5 minutes)

11. After you have entered or checked a batch, who else looks at it? **P** What do they see? How do they
    know what you checked? **L** audit needs, accountability, what a record of review should hold.
12. Whose name is on a mistake? **L** who bears the risk, and so who decides.

## F. What may leave the building (5 minutes)

13. Are there documents you could not show to an outside service? Which, and why? **P** Who decided? What
    would make an exception possible? **L** the real boundary for ADR-008's tiers; the words they use for it.

## G. Role modules (use the one that fits, 5 to 10 minutes)

**Accounts payable.** How do you match an invoice with an order and a delivery? What happens to an invoice
with no match? How do you handle a supplier who changes their layout? How are duplicates found?

**Bookkeeper.** How do you handle a shoebox of receipts at month-end? Which fields do you need from a
receipt, and which do you ignore? How are different clients' rules kept apart? What do you do with a
faded till receipt?

**Identity verification.** What do you check on a document, in what order? How do you use the machine
readable zone? What happens if the zone and the printed text disagree? How do you decide a document is
fake or altered? What would you never leave to a machine?

**Owner.** How much of this do you do yourself? What would you pay for it not to be your evening? What does
a mistake in a tax or payment record cost you?

## H. Closing (5 minutes)

14. If you could change one thing about how this work gets done, what would it be?
15. What did I not ask that I should have?
16. Is there someone who does this work in a different way, who would be willing to talk to me?

Tell them what the project is, in two sentences, only now. Ask for a first reaction and write it down as
*said*, separately from everything above, because it is a reaction to a pitch and not an observation of
work. Thank them. Remind them of the withdrawal period.

## After the session

- Clean the notes the same day, while the tone is still remembered.
- Write the one-paragraph summary and the five most surprising things.
- Mark each observation *said*, *seen* or *inferred*.
- Note anything the guide made awkward, and change the guide after the first three sessions, not before.

## Do not

- ask "would you use ...?", "would you pay ...?" or "do you like ...?": the answers predict nothing;
- ask two questions at once;
- correct a participant's description of how their tool works;
- take a photograph of a document, or write down values from it.
